# What would make a matrix statistic a vacuum energy? {#космологическая-постоянная}

A clock register contains a normalized seven-by-seven matrix $\rho$. Someone asks how much energy its state contributes to the expansion of the Universe. One can calculate a number from the matrix immediately. Giving that number units of energy per volume is where the physical model begins.

<!-- truncate -->

## Where the sign enters {#почему-больше-нуля}

Take the purity $P=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^2)$. Its excess above $1/7$ is exactly the squared Frobenius distance from the maximally mixed state. That quantity is positive for every other state. Now suppose a field model identifies the vacuum-energy density with $\rho_{\rm vac}=C(P-1/7)$. The sign of the excess is fixed by linear algebra. The sign and units of $C$, the state realized in nature and the coupling to gravity all enter through the proposed model. Even a constant energy shift leaves the original matrix statistic unchanged. [Positive-energy readout](/docs/physics/gravity/cosmological-constant#теорема-лямбда-положительна)

## A numerical test of suppression {#шесть-механизмов}

One proposed contribution comes from summing over integer winding configurations with different phases. Opposite phases can cancel. The question is how much weight the cancelling terms actually carry.

For the specified lattice cost $S_0=20$, the zero winding has weight one. The nearest nonzero configurations have small weight and the same phase. Including the zero winding, the relative reduction of the sum's magnitude is about $1.34\times10^{-16}$: the ratio to the unweighted sum is almost one. Omitting zero from both sums changes the relative reduction to about $1.55\times10^{-9}$, still leaving a ratio very close to one. These numbers test a proposed cancellation at a stated cost. Converting either sum to a physical energy requires an independently derived spectrum, measure and normalization. [Winding-sum calculation](/docs/physics/dual-aspect/zeta-regularization)

Other budget estimates combine assumptions about correlations, renormalization and supersymmetry. Agreement with measured vacuum energy would require the same physical model to fix the inputs independently of the density being tested. At present UHM has exact matrix identities and conditional field-model estimates. The value and sign of the cosmological constant remain open.
