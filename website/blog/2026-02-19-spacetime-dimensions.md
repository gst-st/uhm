# Can a clock and three charges describe spacetime? {#почему-пространство-трёхмерно-а-время-одномерно}

Two stations prepare exactly the same quantum state. A comparison of their density matrices gives zero distance. A pulse takes five ticks to go from one station to the other. Both measurements make sense. They answer different questions. Which construction in UHM could recover the distance the pulse measures?

<!-- truncate -->

## What the three coordinates measure {#откуда-берётся-тройка}

Choose an octonionic unit and three commuting rotation charges. Their joint fluctuations in a growing array of registers fill three-dimensional coordinate space. Adjoining a single point at infinity gives a space with the topology of a three-sphere. A depth clock supplies the other coordinate when its ticks become fine and the ends of its record recede. The resulting mathematical space is $\mathbb R\times S^3$. Two of the three charges measure colour degrees of freedom. No ranging experiment has identified those coordinates with the positions of stations. [Charge spectrum and depth clock](/docs/proofs/physics/emergent-manifold)

## What two probes can test {#о-чём-молчит-теория}

There is also a construction that starts with the station experiment. Record which transfers a probe can make, and how many ticks each consumes. If usable transfers can be relayed and zero cost separates distinct stations, their minimum costs satisfy a directed triangle inequality. Reciprocal costs make the distance symmetric.

A three-station model shows why using two probes matters. Successive segments take two and three ticks for both probes in one model. In another, the second probe takes two and four. No single change of clock units aligns the two readings in the latter case: a clock that agrees on the first segment still disagrees on the second. [First-arrival experiment](/docs/geometry/response-metric)

Periodic networks with specified transfer and refinement rules realize a circle and a product of circles as limits. Recovering physical spacetime would require independently calibrated probes to agree across scales, together with a causal structure and dynamics that reproduce the measured geometry. A count of internal coordinates settles none of those experimental questions. [Common limiting distance](/docs/geometry/common-limit)
