# Breath following and breath counting · protocol 1

**Status:** prospective design, executable analysis and synthetic controls. No human observations are included. The confirmatory unit is an independently recruited person randomly assigned to one instruction. All times use one monotonic acquisition clock. Python 3.11+ with its standard library runs the instrument and analysis.

## Question and prediction

Can the same two exercises preserve present respiratory phase equally well while preserving different amounts of information about past breaths? The target population for this protocol is consenting Russian-speaking adults aged 18 or older with at most five lifetime sessions of either exercise who can use the two response buttons. Each person participates once, individually. Eligibility is determined before allocation. The effect is the difference in mean accuracy per assigned person under these instrumented instructions. The study measures immediate performance during one session. Training effects, experiences of accomplished practitioners and spiritual attainment require other designs.

The count arm silently numbers **completed** exhalations from one to ten, repeats, and begins with one on the next completion after noticing a lost count. The follow arm attends to the felt beginning, progress and end of natural inhalation and exhalation without deliberately keeping a numerical tally, returning to the breath after noticing distraction. Use the same chair, environment, unconstrained natural breathing, response device, masking of the clock and identical session length/instruction delivery. Eon Zen describes both exercises as successive stages. Random parallel assignment, aligning the count to the completion, trial start tones and questions are laboratory adaptations. [Eon Zen instructions](https://eonzen.org/how-to-meditate) · [Engakuji on the variants](https://note.com/engakuji/n/nc42f68093596?hl=en) · [Prior breath-count measurement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25386148/)

## Feasibility before the confirmatory cohort

The phase interval can fit inside the 20-point equivalence band only if the two arms' fractions of unknown phase trials sum to less than 0.08305 (about 4.15% in each arm when equal). This follows by adding the two widths from missingness and two confidence radii. Before allocating the 2,800-person cohort, run a separate masked equipment/usability pilot with 20 people per arm and the same trial quotas. The custodian counts unknown phase records in each anonymous arm; proceed only if each count is at most 6 of 320. Check physical clock loopback, phase polarity and response mapping against the bench fixture. The pilot supplies neither effect estimates nor data to the confirmatory analysis. If equipment or wording changes, version and re-lock the protocol and repeat the feasibility gate with new participants. A successful pilot cannot guarantee precision in the later cohort.

## Fixed session

1. Independently qualify and consent the cohort, before revealing any assignments. Record the neutral 60-second calibration with no counting or questions and verify the instrument. Lost calibration, dropout, protocol deviation or change of strategy after randomization stays in the assigned denominator. Participants may stop at any time. Obtain applicable human-research review before running sessions. Store consent and identity separately from pseudonymized research files.
2. The allocation custodian fixes 2,800 unique pseudonyms and a single independently generated 32-byte secret. Draw the secret without access to outcomes, commit the original ID-file and secret digests before generating assignments, and publish the resulting protocol commitment in a timestamped independent registry before the first assigned session. Keep future questions, targets, the roster and the secret concealed. Reveal the secret for verification after data lock. A Git commit identifies the design version; external registration and actual enrollment are future steps.
3. Give the assigned recorded instruction for three minutes of familiarization, with equal-duration recordings. Give both groups the same description of both questions and buttons and two labelled button demonstrations on the screen. No scored practice, accuracy feedback, count display, clocks, pacing or correction. Run four blocks of eight trials with two-minute neutral rests. Keep question type hidden until the probe. Retain original assignment and all 32 slots after interruption, withdrawal or missing responses; no replacement subjects or trials. Both arms get an identical neutral follow-up asking about silent counting, timing strategies, losing the thread, restarting and their own description. Store these verbatim, separately. They are exploratory and cannot change the primary sample.
4. At the start marker both groups begin a new segment. In the count arm the next completed exhale is one. In the follow arm the marker directs attention back to the current breath. A probe asks exactly one of two questions in Russian. Phase: «Непосредственно перед сигналом был вдох или выдох?» History: «От начала этого отрезка до сигнала завершилось чётное или нечётное число выдохов?» Equal-size button labels are «вдох / выдох» or «чётное / нечётное». Normal left button is inhale/even; reverse for half of each arm. The signal waveform is identical for both questions; the display and response clock start at the physical cue. Only the first button edge after cue is registered. Log the physical left/right edge. The sealed analysis reconstructs the semantic response from the committed mapping and scheduled question. No additional opportunity on a miss.
5. Each subject has four repetitions of every phase × queried feature × physical parity combination. Even target counts are 4, 6, 8 and 10. Odd targets are 3, 5, 9 and 11. Their means are both seven. The four exact counts appear once per assigned cell in a keyed pseudorandom order independently of arm. This crosses physical truth with question and includes wrap-around at ten. Equal mean breath count does not balance every elapsed duration. The two-minute acquisition timeout starts at trial arming, separately from the five-second answer window.

## Independent reference and temporal cues

Record the expansion-positive belt in integer ADC units at 100 Hz with raw samples, arming timestamps, stimulus loopback on the acquisition timebase and physical button edges. Keep recording beyond the query through at least the next phase onset. Use the same audio settings, sensor orientation and comfortable sitting posture in both conditions. Establish polarity and common timestamping at instrument setup; a dummy run with known phase and count must pass before enrolment. Never pace or hold the participant's breath.

`acquisition.py` locks a causal ten-sample moving sum. Its reversal threshold is one tenth of the baseline 90th–10th percentile span on that scale, rounded up and bounded below by one. Four complete cycles are required to estimate the timing comparator. Arm each new trial with a fresh detector. Request the anchor 250 ms after detection of an inhale if the phase persists. After physical delivery of the anchor, count subsequent detected inhale onsets as completed respiratory cycles. Request the probe 250 ms into the assigned detected phase after the assigned number of completions. Cancel a pending request if the phase changes. An actual device must acknowledge delivery, or keep the slot missing. A flat baseline, missing sample grid, undelivered/late marker or timeout is logged; after a technical failure do not silently interpolate or rerun an assigned slot. An actual late marker is still scored against the independent physiological reference and the fixed quality rules. Archive timeout as an end event with no delivered cue; preserve detector requests separately from delivery receipts. The provided replay runs the same controller with zero actuator latency for bench testing. Hardware timing and rater reliability have not been validated on people here.

Two independent annotators receive only raw respiratory traces and opaque trial boundaries, without arms, targets, questions, answers, other annotations or predicted onset times. Each marks each start of inspiration and expiration and flags poor contact, indeterminate pauses or motion as artifacts. For a prolonged invalid interval mark each affected respiratory phase including the bracket covering a marker; retain original interval annotations in the raw archive. Use actual recorded anchor/cue afterwards to align the two locked readings. An anchor must fall inside inspiration. A qualifying window needs alternating phase onsets spanning before anchor to after cue, with each marker at least 150 ms from a phase boundary. Count inhale onsets strictly inside (anchor, cue), each denoting completion of the preceding expiration. Both readers must agree on exact count and terminal phase. Conflicts, artifacts, truncated brackets, incorrect targets and physical responses outside (0, 5000] ms give an unknown primary outcome with its own reason. Retain the consensus physiology even for a wrong target or absent response. No adjudication using the participant's answer.

A prespecified secondary comparator divides elapsed time (minus the baseline median inspiratory duration for an expiratory probe) by the participant's baseline median complete period, rounds to nearest (half up), and predicts the result's parity. Baseline medians use the lower median. Report its accuracy bounds on all assigned history trials by arm. Also report the in-sample upper score for an oracle using just each person's observed phase and the comparator bit, formed by summing within-cell majority counts. Unknown slots receive the best possible score for that upper bound. That ceiling describes those finite, declared cells; it cannot bound perception of every timing or sensory cue in a person. A difference reproduced by the timing-only rule admits that rival explanation; weak timing performance cannot exclude unmeasured cues.

## Frozen analysis and failure conditions

Set the primary behavioural region before data: count minus follow phase accuracy strictly between −0.10 and +0.10, history accuracy difference strictly greater than +0.10, and both phase accuracies above 0.65. These are instrument and practical-effect choices. The formal derivation predicts the sign under its memory and reporting conditions; it does not supply these numerical effect sizes. Per participant each endpoint has 16 assigned binary trials. Unknowns enter all lower/upper bounds as zero/one respectively. Means weight people equally; no trial is an independent participant. The finite observer ceiling applies to a declared binary report channel; no theoretical chance score is substituted for an absent human response. All randomized subjects, including silent dropouts and strategy changes, remain in the analysis.

Six simultaneous one-sided Hoeffding bounds use total alpha 0.05: both ends for each of two differences and the lower end for the phase accuracy of each arm. For two independent groups of n participants the difference radius is sqrt(log(120)/n), and each arm radius is sqrt(log(120)/(2n)). This uses independent bounded participant scores; multiple responses or repeated sessions from one person must not be substituted. At n = 1,400 per arm the radii are 0.05848 and 0.04135. With complete data, assumed true phase means 0.85 and history advantage 0.25, the joint-support probability is bounded below by 0.8210 under the design assumptions. This is a planning calculation, not an observed effect. [Hoeffding's bounded-variable inequalities](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

`supported` requires the entire phase difference interval inside the margins, the history lower bound above +0.10 and both phase floors cleared. These jointly imply a positive interaction between practice and question. `incompatible` means a primary difference interval excludes the corresponding predicted region. All other outcomes are `inconclusive`. A weak phase assay alone cannot establish or refute the memory explanation. Equality of the two groups on both questions is compatible with incidental retention of one parity bit. Even perfect parity performance cannot establish that a ten-state representation was used. Report all six bounds, counts of each form of missingness, individual-arm denominators and the timing comparator regardless of decision. No outcome peeking, model selection, covariate adjustment, threshold changes, subset redefinition or sample extension for the primary result. A future pilot with usability changes requires a new version and new untouched participants for confirmation.

## Running and auditing a future session

Only pseudonymous records belong in the study directory for a real run. Keep them outside this repository. Commit registry digests before unmasking the arm key. The laboratory custodian can reconstruct the allocation by rerunning the same command with the original inputs in a different empty directory and comparing the manifest digest.

~~~sh
python3 -B study.py plan
python3 -B study.py pilot-lock pilot-ids.csv pilot-secret.bin pilot-run
python3 -B study.py pilot-qc pilot-run PILOT_PROTOCOL_SHA256 PILOT_RECORDS_SHA256 pilot-events.csv pilot-landmarks.csv pilot-baseline.csv pilot-raw-index.csv pilot-raw-store
python3 -B study.py lock participants.csv secret.bin run
python3 -B acquisition.py baseline-trace.csv
python3 -B acquisition.py baseline-trace.csv --replay trial-trace.csv --target-n 5 --target-phase inhale
python3 -B study.py records-digest events.csv landmarks.csv baseline.csv raw-index.csv
python3 -B study.py analyze run PROTOCOL_SHA256 RECORDS_SHA256 events.csv landmarks.csv baseline.csv raw-index.csv raw-store
python3 -B -m unittest discover -v
~~~

The two hashes in the last analysis command must come from the external records. Freeze the raw archive, the blinded raters' annotations, the button/loopback logs and the per-person baseline file and timestamp the records digest before handing the allocation to the analyst. Sensor traces, event loggers and raters have human operators: a hash attests unchanged bytes after the lock and cannot attest their physiological validity.

| File | Required fields and meaning |
|---|---|
| participants.csv | participant; one unique pseudonymous ID per randomized person. |
| run/roster.csv | participant,arm,buttons. Arms are count or follow; mappings are normal or reversed. |
| run/schedule.csv | participant,trial,query,target_phase,target_parity,target_n. Trial is 1..32. No arm column. |
| events.csv | participant,trial,kind,timestamp_ms,value. Kinds anchor/cue/answer/end, at most one per kind; cue carries the actually displayed question (phase or history), answer carries left or right; other values are blank. An unverified or wrong displayed question leaves the trial unknown. Events are actual delivered markers or physical button edges. |
| landmarks.csv | participant,trial,rater,kind,timestamp_ms. Readers a/b; kind inhale/exhale/artifact. Leave unavailable streams absent. |
| baseline.csv | participant,period_ms,inhale_ms from the neutral baseline estimator. Omit unavailable calibration. |
| raw-index.csv | participant,sha256; one nonempty deidentified acquisition bundle per person with any records. Store its original bytes under raw-store/SHA256. Include raw calibrated belt signal, clock/loopback, physical button trace and source annotations. |
| Single belt trace | timestamp_ms,value, ordered integers at exact 10 ms intervals. Output from the replay is a list of requests, never primary observations. |

The instrument reconstruction produces a row for every assignment with observed_phase, completed_exhales, answer, elapsed_ms and reason. Physical observations stay blank if unavailable. The final command verifies the committed schedule, analysis source, locked inputs and archived acquisition bytes. Preserve the reason table alongside the aggregate result; it can also be generated from the decoded button-label file with `measure.py schedule.csv button-labels.csv landmarks.csv observations.csv` and contains no imputed events. The tests use generated traces and synthetic controls only.
