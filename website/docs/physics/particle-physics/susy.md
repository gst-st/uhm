# Parallel spinors and supersymmetry

## Counting preserved spinors {#n1-susy}

The real eight-dimensional spin representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(7)$ restricts to $\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf7$ under $G_2$. For a seven-manifold with full $G_2$ holonomy and its associated spin structure, the invariant subspace gives one parallel spinor. Compactifying eleven-dimensional supergravity on such a manifold in an unwarped flux-free product background preserves $N=1$ supersymmetry in four dimensions. An internal algebra with automorphism group $G_2$ alone does not specify a spin connection, manifold or supergravity background.

## What determines supersymmetry breaking? {#гипотеза-минимального-суперпотенциала}

<a id="голоморфность-суперпотенциала"></a>

The invariant alternating three-forms on the seven-dimensional fundamental representation form a one-dimensional space. A superpotential requires a field space, its representations, holomorphic contractions and coefficients. Even after choosing a nonzero homogeneous cubic $W$, its derivative vanishes at the origin. Thus the existence of a cubic cannot establish a nonzero F-term at the physical vacuum.

## Vacuum and mass scales {#susy-lambda}

A supergravity spectrum depends on the Kähler potential, superpotential and chosen vacuum. Soft masses also depend on how the observable fields couple to the breaking sector. A calculation of vacuum energy requires those same dynamics and a normalization of physical units. The spinor count fixes none of the corresponding masses, energy densities or experimental thresholds. [Physical bridge premises](../../reference/premises).
