# Collective Organization and Consciousness

<a id="исторический-контекст"></a><a id="мотивация"></a>

A pair of musicians can synchronize with the same metronome, or one can adjust to the other. Their timing can look identical until a perturbation is delivered to one musician alone. The first case tests a common cause, the second additionally allows information transfer. Neither timing record identifies a single subject who experiences the duet. Such an identification needs a separate account of who can report what.

<a id="составная-система"></a><a id="определение-коллективного-бессознательного"></a>

## What can be learned from joint records?

If a joint quantum preparation $\rho_{AB}$ is part of the model, its marginals are partial traces. Even complete knowledge of both marginals does not determine the correlations. For example, prepare two fair classical bits equal in one protocol and opposite in another. Each person sees a fair bit. Comparing their records distinguishes the preparations perfectly, with no entanglement or shared perspective required.

In the quantum representation the total correlation $I(A:B)=S(\rho_A)+S(\rho_B)-S(\rho_{AB})$ vanishes exactly on product states. A shared random cause can make it positive. A coupling can also leave a product state unchanged, for example if its Hamiltonian commutes with that state. To distinguish communication from common drive, randomize a local prompt, control access to the cue and record the other member's response. ## Two controls on a group score

For a fixed product basis write $P(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2$, $Q(\rho)=\sum_i\rho_{ii}^2$ and $1+\Phi=P/Q$. Both $P$ and $Q$ multiply on product states. Consequently
$$
1+\Phi(\rho_A\otimes\rho_B)=(1+\Phi(\rho_A))(1+\Phi(\rho_B)).
$$
Two independent pure states $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ each have $\Phi=1$, and their product has $\Phi=3$. The score grows although the mutual information remains zero.

An unconstrained aggregation channel can also manufacture a group score. A reset $X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma$ gives the same chosen score for every group. A mean of the marginals is reproducible but assigns the same state to the equal-bit and opposite-bit protocols above. Preserve the joint record and test in advance which held-out intervention the aggregated statistic predicts. [Measurement and aggregation](/docs/applied/research/reconstruction-identifiability)

## Measuring interpersonal prediction

Collect one person's account privately, and elicit the other's prediction before either can see the report. A shuffled-partner baseline and a baseline matched for the shared stimulus test whether the prediction carries partner-specific information. Accurate prediction and a shared feeling are distinct claims. A phase score $|\sin(\arg\gamma_{ij})|$ changes with the basis phase reference. Interpreting it as empathy requires a calibrated observation map and an independently measured outcome. [Phase references](/docs/physics/dual-aspect/gap-semantics)

## Recurrence and transmission

Recurring images, stories or answers may reflect shared stimuli, copying, a common task or repeated learning. To test transmission, record exposure and timing, vary who receives the information first, and score later responses on material excluded from calibration. To test a group acting as one observer, specify its input and output ports, which histories remain accessible to the group, and a prediction that differs from a coordinated set of individual observers. The phenomenal interpretation remains open. [Practice, records and observers](/docs/consciousness/empirical/breath-observers)
