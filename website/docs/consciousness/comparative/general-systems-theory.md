# General Systems Theory and Coherence Cybernetics

General systems theory studies organization, feedback and interaction across substrates. A seven-level density-matrix model is one way to represent selected operations. Calling it a generalization requires a map from another model that preserves the experiments and quantities being compared.

## Feedback and composition

For an open system specify the input, update and return channel. For two connected systems specify how an output of the first becomes an input of the second. A correspondence of labels such as “boundary” or “feedback” permits a comparison. To test it, prepare a state, intervene on a link and determine whether both descriptions predict the same joint record.

## A counterexample to a fixed universal dimension

Take a classical register with eight perfectly distinguishable states and a simultaneous readout that names the state without error. To preserve that experiment with a single quantum register, states with different labels must have orthogonal supports. A seven-dimensional Hilbert space has at most seven nonzero mutually orthogonal supports. A seven-level readout can represent some tasks by identifying states; the eight-label experiment prevents that identification. A composition-preserving map must state what its domain retains.
