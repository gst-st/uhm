# Particle-physics hypotheses and possible tests

The operational network results establish covariance for specified link and matter actions. They leave the microscopic group, field spectrum and couplings open. Each row below therefore describes a **conditional field-model comparison**. A result can test its premise once the parameters, uncertainty and decision criterion have been fixed independently of the tested observation. [Network results](/docs/reference/network-results) · [Physical bridge premises](/docs/reference/premises).

| Observable | What the model must specify before the test | Independent observation |
|---|---|---|
| Muon magnetic anomaly $a_\mu$ | Select an SM hadronic calculation with uncertainties. A new-mediator alternative supplies its mass and muon coupling. | Compare the two calculated intervals with a withheld measurement of $a_\mu$. |
| Charged-lepton flavour violation | A model with only light massive neutrinos uses independently determined mixing and masses. A heavy-mediator alternative requires flavour couplings or effective-operator coefficients. | Predict branching fractions or a conversion rate for a specified nucleus. Compare with separate decay or conversion samples. |
| Lepton universality in $B$ decays | Fix the electron and muon Wilson coefficients, photon treatment and invariant-mass bin. The SM prediction includes lepton-mass and radiative effects. | Measure $R_K$ or $R_{K^*}$ in an unused sample or bin with the specified event definition. |
| Electron electric dipole moment | Specify CP-violating phases, interactions and masses, then calculate the dipole coefficient. Counting Higgs doublets leaves other possible sources of CP violation open. | Compare the predicted interval with an independent EDM measurement. |
| Neutrinoless double beta decay | For light Majorana exchange specify the masses, mixing, Majorana phases and nuclear matrix element. Phase cancellations affect the rate. | Compare a predicted half-life for a named isotope with an independent exposure. Sensitivity must reach the stated interval to exclude it. |
| Proton decay | Supply a baryon-number-violating operator, mediator mass, couplings, running and hadronic matrix elements. For dominant dimension-six exchange a partial lifetime scales as $M_X^4$ at fixed couplings. | Test the predicted channel ratio or partial lifetime with independent decay searches. |
| Dark-matter direct detection | A recoil model needs the species' local density, velocity distribution, mass and interaction with the target. A relic mass or axion fraction alone leaves the event rate undetermined. | Compare a predicted recoil spectrum with independent detector data after applying the specified response. |
| Higgs self-coupling | Fix the scalar potential, field normalization and electroweak inputs. A shift in the trilinear coupling also requires a separately fixed coefficient. | Compare the resulting double-Higgs rate or distribution with a withheld sample, accounting for other contributing couplings. |
| Neutrino mass ordering | Supply a mass model, for example $m_D$ and $M_R$ in a type-I seesaw, with parameters determined independently of the ordering test. | Compare the predicted sign of the atmospheric splitting, in a stated convention, with an independent oscillation spectrum. |
| Light sterile neutrinos | Specify additional singlet fields, mass splittings and active–sterile mixing. A count of three clock harmonics places no bound on gauge singlets. | Test the predicted baseline and energy dependence in an independent appearance or disappearance sample. |
| Extra gauge bosons | Choose the additional gauge sector, breaking scale, charges and widths. T-297 and T-329 concern a specified representation and its extension. | Test the resulting neutral dilepton or charged lepton–neutrino spectrum over a stated mass interval. |
| Charged Higgs boson | One electroweak doublet with the usual symmetry breaking leaves no physical charged scalar. An extension supplies the scalar spectrum and couplings. T-296 concerns the choice of field content. | Test a specified production rate times branching fraction for a charged scalar in a withheld sample. |

A value selected using a published signal or limit is a calibration for the next test. For example, a bound used to choose $M_X$ cannot independently validate the resulting lifetime. The field models and their inputs are discussed in [Neutrino masses](/docs/physics/particle-physics/neutrino-masses), [Proton decay](/docs/physics/particle-physics/proton-decay), [Dark matter](/docs/physics/cosmology-phys/dark-matter) and [Higgs sector](/docs/physics/particle-physics/higgs-sector).
