# Diagnosis with Uncertainty

A diagnostic verdict concerns a specified model and observation law. Physical measurements follow [the identifiability protocol](/docs/applied/research/reconstruction-identifiability); computations use [the state-preserving scheme](./implementation). The seven-component stress panel is selected [D] and is not equivalent to viability or the full window. The universal T-92 and T-102 statements in their former scope are withdrawn.

## Input checks

The record includes the frame, density state, dynamical and control families, preparation, time calibration, experiential extension, and readout. Observations carry a law $p_\rho(y\mid u)$ and a confidence set $K$ of all admissible records. Check matrix Hermiticity, positivity, and trace. Data that do not identify a state on observation-law fibres do not establish a unique verdict.

## Four gate margins

$$
m(\rho)=(P-2/7,\ R-1/3,\ \Phi-1,\ D_{\rm diff}-2).
$$

The first margin must be strictly positive; the others nonnegative. Certifying Cap2 requires all four inequalities for every record in $K$. If records in $K$ have different verdicts, the outcome is **unresolved**. If every record fails at least one condition, Cap2 is rejected within the model. That does not mean biological death or absence of experience without a separate bridge.

Name the proxy $D^{7D}=1+6\mathrm{Coh}_E$ explicitly. It does not replace an experiential entropy readout. $C=\Phi R\ge1/3$ is necessary but insufficient for the two scalar access gates. Phase Gap, purity alone, and maximal stress alone do not determine the complete verdict.

## Robustness of a verdict

For valid states with $\|\rho-\widehat\rho\|_F\le\delta$, $|P-\widehat P|\le2\delta$, $|R-\widehat R|\le14\delta$, and $|\Phi-\widehat\Phi|\le112\delta$. These conservative bounds use the quadratic identities, $P,d\ge1/7$, and difference-of-quotient estimates. Experiential readout requires its own regularity bound. A positive margin exceeding the corresponding error certifies a robust numerical conclusion.

Phase statistics require an amplitude floor on the measured entries: phase is undefined at zero and its error is not controlled by matrix norm alone. Small coherences must not produce a falsely confident phase verdict.

## Dynamic viability

The static domain $P>2/7$ is selected by the majority convention. A dynamic conclusion specifies the admissible domain, controls, disturbances, and time horizon. Prove invariance or a viability kernel with the required quantifier order. Distance to a static shell does not guarantee survival of an arbitrary controlled process.

Near a hyperbolic equilibrium analyse the full Jacobian on Hermitian trace-zero space. A linear dissipator's spectral gap does not replace this Jacobian. Nonnormal generators require a prefactor in exponential estimates; negative spectral real parts alone do not control transient amplification in the selected norm.

## Intervention and causality

An intervention specifies an admissible model change. Additional GKSL terms are permitted with nonnegative rates; a selected three-group description is not exhaustive uniqueness. Loss minimization guarantees safety only when a safe action exists and the full trajectory constraint has been proved.

A causal test fixes the target, gate, dissipation, inputs, resources, and preparation or models their joint variation. Correlation with recovery does not prove causation. A positive baseline rate cannot start $I/7$ through a closed gate.

## Examples and reporting

A synthetic matrix is a mathematical example, not a patient, organization, or conscious agent. Arbitrarily assigning symptoms to matrix entries cannot establish clinical classifications or scales. Physical use requires independent calibration and comparison with alternatives.

Report raw observations, model, parameters, frame, confidence set, four margins, numerical errors, and the precise scope of the conclusion. Withdrawn universal proofs cannot serve as confirmation.
