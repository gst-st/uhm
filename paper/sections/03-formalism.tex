\section{Geometry of the chosen seven-level register}\label{sec:geometry}
Take a complex Hilbert space of dimension seven, a specified orthonormal basis, and a trace-one positive semidefinite operator $\rho\in\D_7$. These are modelling premises. Interpreting $\Tr(E\rho)$ as an outcome probability adds the Born pairing with a physically realized effect $0\le E\le I$. Quantum reconstruction theorems impose further operational principles on compositions and measurements \citep{chiribella2011operational}. Here one can ask which quantities the chosen register and instruments determine.

There are seven real diagonal entries with unit sum and 21 complex off-diagonal entries. The full-rank states have 48 independent real coordinates. Define
\begin{equation}
 p_i=\rho_{ii},\qquad P=\Tr(\rho^2),\qquad Q=\sum_i p_i^2,
 \qquad \Phi=\frac{P-Q}{Q},\qquad R=\frac1{7P} .
\label{eq:scores}
\end{equation}
Here $R$ is a purity proxy. The accuracy of an implemented self-model is a separate measured quantity. $\Phi$ refers to the selected frame.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp geometry]\label{prop:geometry}
For every seven-level state,
\[
 \tfrac17\le Q\le P\le1,\qquad
 P-\tfrac17=\|\rho-I/7\|_{\rm HS}^2,\qquad
 0\le\Phi\le7P-1.
\]
At each allowed purity both endpoints of the $\Phi$ inequality are attained, even within a single unitary orbit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz on the populations gives $Q\ge1/7$. Hermiticity gives $P-Q=2\sum_{i<j}|\rho_{ij}|^2$. The eigenvalues are nonnegative and sum to one, so their squared sum is at most one. Expanding the square about $I/7$ proves the identity. For any spectrum, the diagonal representative has $Q=P$. Conjugation by the discrete Fourier matrix gives uniform populations, since every matrix entry of that unitary has squared modulus $1/7$. Hence $Q=1/7$ on the same orbit. All purities occur along the line joining $I/7$ to a pure projector.
\end{proof}

A coordinate readout recovers the populations. For each pair $i,j$, project instead onto $(\ket{i}\pm\ket{j})/\sqrt2$ and record the two probabilities and the complementary outcome. Their difference is $2\operatorname{Re}\rho_{ij}$. Using $(\ket{i}\mp i\ket{j})/\sqrt2$ gives $2\operatorname{Im}\rho_{ij}$. Phase-sensitive tomography requires calibrated phase references and repeated preparations. If only the seven diagonal Fano-line effects are available, $q=Bp/3$ for their point--line incidence matrix $B$. The identities $B^\top B=2I+\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ and $\sum p_i=1$ recover $p=(3B^\top q-\mathbf1)/2$, while all 42 off-diagonal real coordinates are absent from this measurement.

\paragraph{Spectrum and phase.}
For strictly positive populations the two-coordinate minor gives $|\rho_{ij}|^2\le p_i p_j$. An individual phase changes under rephasing the basis; a phase around a cycle of nonzero entries is invariant. With complete support on seven axes there are $21-7+1=15$ independent cycle angles. A spanning tree fixes six edge phases, leaving one angle per remaining edge. These counts concern observables in a specified frame. They give no enumeration of human qualities.

\paragraph{Metric information.}
On neighbouring full-support diagonal states with populations $p$ and $p+dp$, the squared Bures distance is $2-2\sum_i\sqrt{p_i(p_i+dp_i)}$. Its second-order term is $\tfrac14\sum_i(dp_i)^2/p_i$, since $\sum_i dp_i=0$. The factor of one quarter specifies the metric normalization. Monotone metrics on density matrices form a family \citep{petz1996}; statistical distinguishability requires a declared measurement model \citep{braunstein1994}. The finite-dimensional Bures topology agrees with the usual topology of density matrices. Its open sets alone cannot select this particular metric tensor, a Born pairing, or a physical algebra of observables.
