\section{A conditional capability gate and its dynamics}
Write the full record as $z=(\rho,\mathsf E,\mathsf M,\mathsf Q)$, with a chosen auxiliary realization or proxy, an implemented self-model, and declared probes and calibration. The corpus defines a cumulative capability level L2 by an L1 requirement and four cuts:
\begin{equation}
 P>2/7,\qquad R\ge1/3,\qquad \Phi\ge1,\qquad D(z)\ge2.
\label{eq:cuts}
\end{equation}
In the seven-level proxy mode let $E$ mark one fixed axis. Write
\[
 C_E=\frac{p_E^2+2\sum_{j\ne E}|\rho_{Ej}|^2}{P}.
\]
In this mode L1 means $C_E>0$, and $D=1+6C_E$. In the extension mode an actual auxiliary register with a specified preparation and normalized readout $\rho_E$ is required; L1 means positive von Neumann entropy, and $D=\exp S(\rho_E)$ with natural logarithms. An absent register or uncalibrated readout leaves its value unknown. For the same native $\rho$, two product extensions $\rho\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}$ and $\rho\otimes I_6/6$ give $D=1$ and $6$ respectively. A named native basis vector does not specify that factor.

\begin{proposition}[Window under the chosen cuts]
Every state passing \eqref{eq:cuts} satisfies $2/7<P\le3/7$, $1/3\le R<1/2$ and $1\le\Phi\le2$. The purity window alone is insufficient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use $R=1/(7P)$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry}. For an explicit pair take $\rho_t=(1-t)I/7+t\ket{+}\bra{+}$ with $\ket{+}=7^{-1/2}\sum_i\ket{i}$ and $0\le t\le1$. Its eigenvalues are $(1+6t)/7$ and $(1-t)/7$ with multiplicity six. Thus $P=(1+6t^2)/7$, $Q=1/7$, $\Phi=6t^2$ and $C_E=(1+12t^2)/(7(1+6t^2))$. Throughout $1/6<t^2\le1/3$ the proxy gate passes. Diagonalize this state in the measurement frame: the new preparation has the same spectrum and $R$, but $\Phi=0$. Figure~\ref{fig:controls} gives one interior example.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Condition for a feedback claim.}
Let $\sigma=\ket j\bra j$ with $j\perp E$. The replacement channel $T(X)=\Tr(X)\sigma$ sends every state to $\sigma$ in one step and keeps it there. Hence even a globally attracting state with $P=1$ can have $C_E=0$. At the opposite extreme $\sigma=\ket E\bra E$ gives positive $C_E$ with all off-diagonal couplings zero. A lower bound on required $E$-feedback must follow from a particular controller, admissible input and noise classes, and a stability inequality. Neither positive support nor that additional dynamical bound identifies subjective experience.

\paragraph{Fixed channels and feedback resources.}
For a fixed CPTP channel $\Lambda$, state $\sigma$ and $0<k\le1$, the update $T(X)=(1-k)\Lambda(X)+k\Tr(X)\sigma$ is CPTP. On states, $T(\rho)-T(\eta)=(1-k)(\Lambda(\rho)-\Lambda(\eta))$. Trace-distance contraction gives a Lipschitz constant at most $1-k$ and a unique fixed state. That state equals $\sigma$ when $\Lambda(\sigma)=\sigma$. A fixed bath, a fixed joint channel and a partial trace yield an affine update. Selecting the bath or the coefficient as a function of an unknown input requires a physical estimation or feedback resource. For a specified Markovian semigroup the GKSL generator is standard quantum structure \citep{lindblad1976semigroup}. Turning off dissipative rates and feedback leaves the unitary von Neumann equation on the supplied space.

For instance, take a purely dephasing generator with the seven coordinate projectors and the seven normalized Fano-line operators $\Pi_\ell/\sqrt3$, all at rate $\gamma/7$. For diagonal Hermitian jumps an entry $\rho_{ij}$ decays at half the rate times the sum of squared jump-eigenvalue differences. Two coordinate projectors and four lines distinguish any given pair; the squared differences sum to $2+4/3$. The off-diagonal decay rate is therefore $5\gamma/21$ and the populations are fixed. This is a model prediction at a measured $\gamma$. The choice of this dissipator and the relation to any physical decoherence rate require calibration.

A concrete resource result uses $\rho_y=(1-y)I/d+y\Theta$, a known pure target $\Theta$, a source producing fresh independent copies with the same unknown $y\in[0,1]$, and fixed $0\le\mu<1$. For the target on this ray with output coordinate $g(y)=\mu+(1-\mu)(d-1)y^3/[1+(d-1)y^2]$, a finite fixed instrument using at most $m$ inputs per attempt has a polynomial unconditional output probability of degree at most $m$. The target coordinate has a noncancelling denominator. No such deterministic exact realization exists. A success record permits a ratio of polynomials: for $d\ge3$ the sharp minimum is three copies if $\mu\ge(2d-3)/[(d-1)(d+3)]$, and four below it. These bounds require positive success probability over the entire ray. The \source{feedback.md}{proof with the complete instrument and matching lower bound} specifies the resource being counted.
