A state estimate can predict a future response while leaving other properties of the preparation undetermined. We characterize exactly which attributions can be recovered from a specified family of interventions and readings. We then examine the seven-level model of Unitary Holonomic Monism. We derive sharp bounds on purity and basis-dependent coherence, give states attaining the proposed capability window, and construct a stable reset with zero support on the marked axis. Fixed channels, conditional instruments and feedback with state-dependent parameters have different implementation requirements. An exact two-rebit circuit shows how a declared interaction can expose a distinction invisible to local tests. For comparisons between observers we derive an error bound on three translations and propose a binocular-rivalry experiment in which two models predict different ranges for the same calibrated contrast. These results establish mathematical and operational properties of specified systems. The physical realization of the register and its interpretation as experience remain empirical and philosophical questions.
