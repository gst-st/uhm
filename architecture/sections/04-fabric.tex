\section{The fabric: seven buses, not a crossbar}
\label{sec:fabric}

\subsection{The interconnect is the seven lines}

Twenty-one pairwise couplings among seven sectors would, on a conventional
fabric, demand a $\binom{7}{2}=21$-edge network or a $7\times7$ crossbar. The
collapse (Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}, L1) replaces this with the seven lines
of $\mathsf{N}$ as \textbf{seven triple-buses}. Every pair of sectors shares
exactly one bus (Steiner $S(2,3,7)$); every sector sits on exactly three
buses; any two buses intersect in exactly one sector. These are not design
targets --- they are theorems of the projective plane, and they hand the
architecture three properties for free:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Uniform load, by symmetry.} Each sector is on exactly three
        buses because $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ acts $2$-transitively on the seven
        points; load balancing is a consequence of the automorphism group,
        not a scheduler.
  \item \textbf{Guaranteed rendezvous, by geometry.} Any two buses meet in a
        sector, so any two sectors that must exchange state have a
        deterministic meeting point --- there is no unreachable pair and no
        routing search.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic repair, by the closure law.} If the direct bus
        for $(i,j)$ is degraded, the state regenerates through the
        \emph{mediator} $k^\ast(i,j)$ --- the third sector of their line ---
        and only through it. Fallback routing needs no tables: the next hop
        is computed from the pair.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Third-order routing and exact Fano pinching contraction}

A conventional router moves data pairwise and is unable to see the third-order
line structure it is carrying: any equal-weight pairwise fabric is a function of
$J-I$ alone and cannot distinguish the Fano wiring from unstructured all-to-all.
\TALOS{} routes on \emph{triples}: a transfer is an interaction
$(i,j\mid k^\ast)$ mediated by the line's unique third sector. Because every
distinct pair of sectors in $\Fano$ shares exactly one common line (Steiner
$S(2,3,7)$), there are no ``unlinked'' pairs; instead, the exact Fano geometry
governs coherence attenuation directly through the Fano pinching channel.

\begin{claimbox}[Fano pinching contraction \status{T}]
Let $\Pi_\ell = \sum_{k\in\ell}\ket{k}\bra{k}$ be the rank-$3$ projector onto
Fano line $\ell$ ($\ell=1,\dots,7$), and define the Fano pinching channel
$\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam) = \tfrac{1}{3}\sum_{\ell=1}^{7}\Pi_\ell\Gam\Pi_\ell$.
By the Steiner $S(2,3,7)$ incidence counts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item every diagonal population $\gamma_{ii}$ lies on $3$ lines and survives
        unchanged: $[\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam)]_{ii} = 3\cdot\tfrac13\gamma_{ii} = \gamma_{ii}$
        (so $P_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is preserved identically);
  \item every distinct pair $(i,j)$ lies on \emph{exactly one} line, so every
        off-diagonal coherence is attenuated by $1/3$:
        $[\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam)]_{ij} = \tfrac13\gamma_{ij}$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, squared off-diagonal coherence contracts by $(1/3)^2 = 1/9$,
giving the exact universal contraction
$\Phi(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam)) = \tfrac{1}{9}\Phi(\Gam)$ for every
$\Gam\in\Dens(\mathbb{C}^7)$. In continuous time, the Fano Lindblad dissipator
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Fano}}[\Gam]=\gamma(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam)-\Gam)
=\tfrac{2}{3}\gamma(\operatorname{diag}(\Gam)-\Gam)$ preserves populations and
damps off-diagonals at uniform rate $2\gamma/3$ (machine-verified in
\texttt{architecture/talos\_v0\_emulator.py} and
\texttt{website/scripts/check\_core\_numbers.py}).
\end{claimbox}

This is the sharp dynamical role of the Fano topology: a single Fano pinching
pass preserves $1/3$ of coherence amplitude ($1/9$ of squared coherence $\Phi$)
rather than destroying coherences completely as full diagonal dephasing does,
and its continuous-time generator sets the exact spectral gap
$\lambda_{\mathrm{deco}}=2/3$ (\S\ref{sec:substrate}).

\begin{trybox}[Try it --- route with one finger]
Put a finger on the canonical Singer Fano figure (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}, right).
Game: carry a message from $S$ to $E$. Rule: you may only travel along a line,
and a pair may only meet \emph{through the third point of its own line}. Find
the line containing both $S$ and $E$ --- it is $\{S,D,E\}$ --- so the message
goes $S\to D\to E$: the mediator is $D$, and there was nothing to look up,
because the pair \emph{names} its own relay. Now break the direct path from
$A$ to $O$ and reroute: their line is $\{O,A,D\}$, so again through $D$ ---
fault repair with no routing table (\S\ref{sec:ecc}). Every one of the $21$
pairs plays the same one-move game; the full mediator table is
Appendix~\ref{app:fano}.
\end{trybox}
