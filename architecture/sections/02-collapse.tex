\section{The architectural collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}

This section states and proves the two results on which the whole design
rests. Both are \status{T}: they rest on established theorems of UHM
together with one elementary Diophantine fact and Hurwitz's theorem. Nothing
here is postulated.

\subsection{The single incidence structure}

Let $\mathsf{N}$ denote the incidence of the Fano plane $\Fano$ in its canonical
Singer cyclic ordering on the seven points $(A,S,D,L,E,U,O) = (1,\dots,7)$, with
seven lines $\{k,k{+}1,k{+}3\}\pmod 7$:
\[
\{A,S,L\},\ \{S,D,E\},\ \{D,L,U\},\ \{L,E,O\},\ \{E,U,A\},\ \{U,O,S\},\ \{O,A,D\},
\]
each line carrying three points, each point lying on three lines, and every
pair of points lying on exactly one common line (the Steiner system
$S(2,3,7)$). Write $k^\ast(i,j)$ for the \emph{mediator} of a pair --- the
unique third point of their common line.

\begin{theorembox}[Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} (Architectural collapse)]
On a seven-mode UHM substrate the four architectural layers --- interconnect,
combinatorial sector-level error code, instruction multiplication support, and
Fano-pinched self-model learning rule --- are functions of the single incidence
$\mathsf{N}$.
\end{theorembox}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:collapse}
Let a holon's state be $\Gam\in\Dens(\mathbb{C}^7)$ evolving under the UHM
Lindbladian $\LindOmega$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item[\textbf{(L1)}] \emph{Interconnect.} The physical coupling is the seven
        lines of $\mathsf{N}$: sector $i$ reaches sector $j$ only through the
        mediator $k^\ast(i,j)$ (the closure law: a coherence
        $\gamma_{ij}$ is created or repaired only by the live arms
        $\gamma_{i k^\ast},\gamma_{k^\ast j}$; two coherences birth a third
        iff the triple is a line).
  \item[\textbf{(L2)}] \emph{Code.} Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the $7\times 7$ Fano
        incidence matrix $M$ (rows $=$ the seven weight-$3$ lines,
        $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(M)=4$) \emph{generates} the
        Hamming code $[7,4,3]$ (16 codewords: $\mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{1}$, the
        seven weight-$3$ lines, and the seven weight-$4$ line complements). Its
        orthogonal $3\times 7$ \emph{parity-check} matrix $H$
        ($\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(H)=3$, $HM^T\equiv 0\pmod 2$) has
        as its rows three independent weight-$4$ line complements spanning the
        dual simplex code $[7,3,4]$; the seven columns of $H$ are the seven
        distinct nonzero vectors of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$, computing the exact
        $3$-bit single-sector fault syndrome.
  \item[\textbf{(L3)}] \emph{Instruction algebra.} The cubic (non-linear)
        part of $\LindOmega$ is supported on the octonionic structure
        constants $f_{ijk}$ --- the unique $G_2$-invariant trilinear form on
        $\mathrm{Im}(\Oct)$ --- which are non-zero exactly when $\{i,j,k\}$ is
        a line (the Fano selection rule). The multiplication support of the
        instruction is the line incidence of $\mathsf{N}$.
  \item[\textbf{(L4)}] \emph{Learning.} The regeneration term $\Regen$ drives
        $\Gam$ toward the anchored self-model
        $\varphi_J(\Gam)=(1-R(\Gam))\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}(\Gam) +
        R(\Gam)\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}$ built from Fano pinching
        $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}$, and both $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Fano}}$
        and the coordinate Fano dissipator $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{Fano}}$ are
        covariant under the discrete collineation group
        $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathsf{N})\cong \mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ (order~$168$) and
        diagonal phase rephasing $U(1)^7\rtimes \mathrm{Aut}(\mathsf{N})$
        (whereas continuous $G_2$ rotates the coordinate basis and does not
        preserve the fixed seven-line Fano partition). The stationary balance
        and repair channels are functions of the same $\mathsf{N}$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence $\mathrm{L1}=\mathrm{L2}=\mathrm{L3}=\mathrm{L4}$ as functions of
$\mathsf{N}$. \hfill$\square$
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Zero mismatch tax]\label{cor:zerotax}
Because the four layers are one structure, there are no inter-layer
interfaces to negotiate. The area, static power, and verification effort a
conventional machine spends reconciling network~$\leftrightarrow$~code,
code~$\leftrightarrow$~ISA, and ISA~$\leftrightarrow$~optimiser
(Table~\ref{tab:four-layers}) is, in \TALOS{}, identically zero: there is
one structure to lay out, verify, and clock.
\end{corollary}

\begin{zerobox}[From zero --- one drawing that is four games]
Chalk a hopscotch grid on the pavement. The same drawing is four different
things at once, depending on what you are doing with it. It is the
\emph{field} --- where your feet may land (the network). It is the
\emph{rule} --- which hops are legal (the instruction). It is the
\emph{referee} --- a wrong hop shows instantly, because your foot is outside
a line (the checking). And it is the \emph{teacher} --- playing the grid over
and over is what makes you better at it (the learning). Nobody bolted four
gadgets together; one chalk drawing does all four jobs \emph{because of its
shape}. Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} says: for a seven-part self-watching
machine there exists exactly one such chalk drawing --- the seven-line figure
on the right of Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} --- and Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}
says no other number of parts has one.
\end{zerobox}

\subsection{Why exactly seven --- and nowhere else}

A skeptic will grant that \emph{if} $N=7$ then the four layers coincide, and
ask whether seven is special or merely one convenient point. It is unique,
and the reason is sharper than a tie-break: binary projective-plane/Hamming
fusion already forces $N=7$ uniquely over all prime powers $q\ge 2$, and
Hurwitz's non-associative division algebra independently lands on the exact same
integer.

\begin{theorembox}[Theorem~\ref{thm:unique} (Uniqueness of the collapse)]
The architectural collapse of Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} is realisable at a
unique node count: $N=7$.
\end{theorembox}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:unique}
Require the binary incidence of an $N$-node projective plane to fuse with a
perfect binary single-error-correcting Hamming code:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item[\textbf{(C1)}] $N=q^2+q+1$ is the point count of a Desarguesian
        projective plane $\mathrm{PG}(2,q)$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ ($q$ a prime
        power, $q\ge 2$), with binary incidence matrix $M\in\mathbb{F}_2^{N\times N}$;
  \item[\textbf{(C2)}] $N=2^r-1$ ($r\ge 2$) and the binary row space of $M$
        generates the Hamming $[2^r{-}1, 2^r{-}1{-}r, 3]$ code (equivalently,
        its dual parity-check kernel $\ker(M)$ has dimension $r > 1$);
  \item[\textbf{(C3)}] \emph{(Compatibility with third-order instruction algebra)}
        $N$ equals the imaginary dimension of a normed division algebra with
        non-zero associator ($\Oct$, Hurwitz's theorem).
\end{itemize}
Then \textbf{(C1)}$\cap$\textbf{(C2)} already forces $N=7$ ($q=2, r=3$)
uniquely over all prime powers $q\ge 2$, and this unique solution coincides
with \textbf{(C3)}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Odd prime powers $q$ fail binary Hamming fusion.}
For every odd prime power $q$, the point count $N=q^2+q+1$ is odd and the
incidence matrix $M$ of $\mathrm{PG}(2,q)$ obeys $MM^T = q I_N + J_N \equiv
I_N + J_N \pmod 2$. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ in odd dimension $N$, the matrix
$I_N+J_N$ has rank $N-1$ (its kernel is the one-dimensional all-ones line
$\{0, \mathbf{1}\}$). Hence $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(M)\ge N-1$
and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\ker(M)\le 1$: the binary incidence code is trivial
and never generates a Hamming $[N, N-r, 3]$ code. In particular, at the bare
integer coincidence $q=5, N=31=2^5-1$, direct Gaussian elimination over
$\mathbb{F}_2$ gives $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(M_{\mathrm{PG}(2,5)})=30$
and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\ker(M_{\mathrm{PG}(2,5)})=1$ (machine-checked in
Lean~4, \texttt{FoundationalBounds.pg2\_5\_refutes\_N31\_hamming\_fusion}, and
in \texttt{website/research/operational-reconstruction/verify\_foundational\_bounds.py}),
rather than the dimension-$26$ / parity-rank-$5$ Hamming $[31,26,3]$ code.

\textbf{Even prime powers $q=2^m$ ($m\ge 1$) force $m=1, r=3, N=7$.}
Substituting $q=2^m$ into $q^2+q+1=2^r-1$ yields
\[
2^{2m}+2^m+2 = 2^r.
\]
For $m\ge 2$, both $2^{2m}$ and $2^m$ are divisible by $4$, so the left-hand
side is congruent to $2\pmod 4$ while $2^r\equiv 0\pmod 4$ for every $r\ge 2$
(and $r=2$ gives $2^{2m}+2^m+2\ge 22 > 4$), a contradiction
(\texttt{FoundationalBounds.pg2EvenHammingUniqueSeven}). Thus $m=1$, $q=2$,
$r=3$, and $N=7$, where $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(M_{\mathrm{PG}(2,2)})=4$
generates the Hamming $[7,4,3]$ code and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\ker(M_{\mathrm{PG}(2,2)})=3$
gives its $3\times 7$ dual simplex $[7,3,4]$ parity-check matrix $H$ ($HM^T\equiv 0\pmod 2$).

\textbf{Coincidence with (C3).} By Hurwitz's theorem the normed division
algebras are $\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\Oct$ of dimensions $1,2,4,8$,
and only $\Oct$ (imaginary dimension $7$) has a non-zero associator. Thus the
unique binary plane-code fusion $N=7$ agrees identically with \textbf{(C3)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No tie-break needed at $N=31$]
The bare arithmetic coincidence $5^2+5+1=31=2^5-1$ does \emph{not} fuse
$\mathrm{PG}(2,5)$ with the binary Hamming $[31,26,3]$ code: over $\mathbb{F}_2$
every odd-order projective plane collapses to rank $N-1$ (a one-dimensional
repetition kernel). Consequently, binary interconnect--ECC fusion alone selects
$N=7$ uniquely across all prime powers $q\ge 2$, and Hurwitz's non-associative
condition \textbf{(C3)} independently lands on the exact same integer $7$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to UHM's operational reconstruction]
In the operational reconstruction of UHM (\texttt{AddressFusion.lean} and
\texttt{DimensionPinch.lean}), $N=7$ and $k=3$ are derived from independent
fault count $N$ and address width $k$ via exact syndrome decoding
($N+1\le 2^k$), bipolar visibility ($2^k\le 8$), and nontrivial perfect
single-fault packing ($N+1\ge 8$). Theorem~\ref{thm:unique} provides a second,
purely projective-coding proof of the same integer $N=7$, verified in Lean~4
with zero custom axioms. \status{T}
\end{remark}
