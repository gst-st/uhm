\section{Composition: ecologies, not megacores}
\label{sec:composition}

The industry's answer to a bigger problem is a bigger machine: more cores,
more layers, one monolithic model scaled without limit. \TALOS{} adopts the
opposite architectural discipline: keep each unified holon tower bounded at a
small, fixed nesting depth and scale out horizontally through peer-to-peer
ecologies.

\begin{claimbox}[Scoped composition depth \status{D} (T-142)]
In the reference \TALOS{}/\SYNARC{} specification, vertical self-model nesting
is capped as a \textbf{scoped model parameter} at depth $d_{\max}=3$ (T-142
\status{D}): a bounded cognitive tower holds four contiguous \DM{} registers
--- base state $\Cog_0$ plus three nested meta-levels $\Cog_1,\Cog_2,\Cog_3$
($4\times 784=3{,}136$~bytes). Earlier claims of a universal impossibility
theorem for depth four (T-136, T-217) were withdrawn; here $d_{\max}=3$ is a
finite design cap motivated by keeping per-agent purity margins, verification
scope, and state footprint strictly bounded, shifting scale-out above depth
three to peer ecologies rather than deeper monolithic nesting.
\end{claimbox}

\begin{zerobox}[From zero --- three dolls deep]
A matryoshka doll inside a doll inside a doll is still \emph{one compact toy}
in your hands. Rather than nesting deeper without end, the design sets a clean
depth cap of three meta-layers (four nested registers in total, base plus
three). Beyond that depth you do not build a taller tower --- you build a
shelf: many complete, bounded toys side by side, talking as peers (an
\emph{ecology}), rather than one unbounded super-doll.
\end{zerobox}

The architectural consequence is precise. A \TALOS{} system scales in two
regimes. \emph{Within} a subject, up to three meta-levels above the base
register ($\Cog_0\to\Cog_1\to\Cog_2\to\Cog_3$), the state is a fixed four-register
tower ($4\times784=3{,}136$~bytes) fitting inside a small slice of L1 cache.
\emph{Above} that design depth, one does not grow a monolithic register file
--- one builds an \textbf{ecology}: many bounded subjects coupled as peers,
coordinating through the \textsc{Fanos} distributed fabric.

Two engineering corollaries follow directly from this bounded design depth.
First, verification is bounded: a single subject's state space is fixed at
$3{,}136$~bytes regardless of the ecology's size, so the formal verification
target does not grow with deployment scale. Second, the failure domain is
bounded: a fault contained in one subject cannot corrupt a monolithic global
state, because coordination above the tower is peer-to-peer \textsc{Fanos}
traffic with sector-level Hamming protection.
